# Conclusions and take-homes

Hopefully you've seen that running regression analysis can be pretty easy, but to do it successfully you do need to think carefully about different aspects:

* How your variables are encoded
* *Which* variables to include, and in particular whether there might be any confounding
* How to interpret the parameter estimates
* Whether results are replicable across different studies or settings.

If you get it right and have enough data you can draw some strong conclusions.  The conclusions are of two types - those you have directly shown and scientific conclusions:

**A direct conclusion** might be something like: O blood group is associated with ~30% reduced risk of severe malaria in this dataset, in a way that is reaonsably consistent across populations and not explained by population structure.

**A scientific conclusion** might be: there's strong evidence that O blood group reduces risk of severe malaria in sub-Saharan African populations.  

We didn't directly show this scientific conclusion, but it's hard to explain the results otherwise.
